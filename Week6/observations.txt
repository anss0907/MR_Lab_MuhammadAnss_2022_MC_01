======================================================
2. OSCILLATIONS AND INSTABILITY
======================================================

Significant oscillation was observed during the obstacle avoidance
behavior when FRONT_THRESHOLD = 0.5 m:

  - The robot repeatedly switched between "turning LEFT" and
    "turning RIGHT" every 200 ms. This occurred because np.min()
    picked the single nearest point in each sector, and sensor noise
    caused the minimum value to alternate between left and right sectors
    frame-by-frame.

  - Example from logs (mode: avoid, threshold 0.5 m):
      Front: 0.48 m → turning LEFT
      Front: 0.47 m → turning RIGHT
      Front: 0.48 m → turning LEFT
      (repeating indefinitely)

  - The robot was essentially spinning in place rather than
    escaping the obstacle.

** SPECIFIC CASE — Two-Pillar Scenario (FRONT_THRESHOLD = 0.5 m) **

  The most notable instability occurred in the TurtleBot3 World when
  the robot entered the gap between two pillars. At this threshold:

  - Both pillars were simultaneously detected within 0.5 m — one
    appearing on the left sector, the other on the right.
  - The left and right minimum distances were nearly equal and flipped
    between frames due to LiDAR noise.
  - The robot could not commit to either direction and entered a
    continuous left-right spin, unable to exit the gap.

  This was clearly visible in the Gazebo simulation — the robot would
  rotate a few degrees left, then immediately reverse to the right,
  oscillating in place indefinitely between the two pillars.

  Root cause: When both sides are equally blocked, np.min() on noisy
  data produces no stable "winning" side, so the decision flips every
  scan cycle (~200 ms). The robot never moves forward or fully turns.

  Fix applied: Reducing FRONT_THRESHOLD to 0.2 m meant the pillars
  were not triggering avoidance until the robot was very close, allowing
  it to pass through the gap in cruise mode before avoidance kicked in.
  Using np.nanmedian() instead of np.min() further stabilized the
  sector readings so the turn direction was consistent once avoidance
  did activate.

Root cause: Using np.min() is highly sensitive to single noisy readings.
Fix applied: Replacing np.min() with np.nanmedian() smooths out
noise and produces stable sector distances that don't flip between
frames.

Wall-follow mode was more stable — the proportional controller
(WALL_KP = 1.5) produced smooth correction for most cases,
with minor angular oscillation (ang_z toggling between ±0.50)
when the right wall had gaps or sharp corners.

======================================================
3. EFFECT OF CHANGING THRESHOLD VALUES
======================================================

FRONT_THRESHOLD changed from 0.5 m → 0.2 m:
  - At 0.5 m: Robot reacted early, large safety margin, but oscillated
    frequently in moderately open spaces (e.g., the world corridors).
  - At 0.2 m: Robot navigated much closer to obstacles before reacting.
    Fewer false triggers in open space. However, reaction time was very
    tight and the robot occasionally cruised into walls before avoidance
    activated (logs showed "Path clear" at Front = 0.20–0.21 m).
  - Recommended value: 0.35 m as a balance between safety and progress.

SIDE_THRESHOLD changed from 0.5 m → 0.2 m:
  - At 0.5 m: Wall-follow activated far from the wall and kept the
    robot roughly centered in corridors.
  - At 0.2 m: Wall-follow only activated when very close to the right
    wall. The robot mostly cruised forward in "clear" mode and rarely
    entered wall-follow mode even in corridors.
  - This reduced the effectiveness of Task 4 behavior.
